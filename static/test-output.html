Interview Prep Cheat Sheet

Role: Senior Full-Stack Engineer | Format: 30-min recruiter screen + behavioral round | Interview date: ~2 weeks

Part A — Top 5 HR / Recruiter Screen Questions

A1. Tell me about yourself / walk me through your background

  • Screening for: Can you clearly summarize your experience, and does it match the role?
  • Answer: I'm a senior full-stack engineer with eight years building production systems in React, TypeScript, and Node. Most recently at Highspot I built an LLM gateway and shipped AI features. Before that at Article I led a Figma-to-code pipeline that powered over ten apps, and at Appnovation I built a COVID tracking system deployed across four provinces. I'm looking for a role where I can keep building high-impact, user-facing products with modern tooling.
  • Don't: Recite your entire resume or go earlier than Appnovation unless asked.

A2. Why are you leaving, or why are you looking now?

  • Screening for: Is this a voluntary move, and are you leaving for the right reasons?
  • Answer: [ADD DETAIL: your actual reason — e.g. layoff, end of contract, seeking growth, team change, company direction]. Keep it neutral and forward-looking. Example if laid off: "Highspot had a reduction in force in early 2026. I'm excited to find a team where I can apply what I learned building AI tooling and scalable React systems."
  • Don't: Badmouth anyone or leave the reason vague.

A3. Why this company / why this role?

  • Screening for: Did you research us, and do you actually care?
  • Answer: [ADD DETAIL: company mission, product you admire, tech stack alignment, team reputation, or growth stage]. Structure: "I'm drawn to [company] because [one specific thing]. The role is a great fit for my background in [React/TypeScript/AI tooling], and I'm excited to [contribute to X or solve Y]."
  • Don't: Give a generic answer that could apply to any company.

A4. What are your salary expectations?

  • Screening for: Are you in budget, and can you talk about money professionally?
  • Answer: [ADD DETAIL: your target range in CAD, e.g. "$135–155k CAD" or "$140k CAD base"]. Say: "Based on my experience and the market for senior full-stack engineers in Vancouver, I'm targeting [range]. I'm flexible depending on the full package — equity, growth, and team fit all matter."
  • Don't: Say "I'm open to anything" or dodge the question entirely.

A5. Logistics: availability, start date, work authorization, location

  • Screening for: Can you actually start when we need you, and are there any blockers?
  • Answer: "I'm authorized to work in Canada, based in Vancouver, and [ADD DETAIL: available immediately / on two weeks' notice / after X date]. I'm open to [remote / hybrid / onsite as posted]."
  • Don't: Be vague about start date or surprise them with a visa need if you're not authorized.

Part B — Top 5 Behavioral Questions

B1. Tell me about your biggest failure (or a time the plan broke)

  • Competency: Self-awareness, learning from mistakes, and resilience.
  • STAR: [ADD DETAIL: no confirmed failure story in the input — add facts before using this in an interview]. Situation: a project or feature that missed the mark or broke. Task: what you owned. Action: what you tried, what went wrong, what you did to recover. Result: what you learned and applied later.
  • Spoken draft: Example skeleton: "At [company], I [describe what failed — a launch that slipped, a technical decision that caused issues]. I realized [what I missed]. I [corrective action]. Since then I [new practice you adopted], and it's helped me [concrete outcome]."
  • Don't: Blame others, pick something trivial, or skip the "what I learned" part.

B2. Tell me about a conflict with a coworker

  • Competency: Collaboration, empathy, and conflict resolution.
  • STAR: [ADD DETAIL: no confirmed conflict story in the input — add facts before using this in an interview]. Situation: disagreement over approach, priority, or ownership. Task: deliver despite tension. Action: how you initiated the conversation, listened, and found common ground. Result: outcome and relationship after.
  • Spoken draft: Example skeleton: "At [company], [coworker] and I disagreed on [technical approach or priority]. I scheduled a one-on-one, asked questions to understand their perspective, and we [compromise or decision]. We shipped [outcome], and our working relationship improved."
  • Don't: Make the other person the villain or end without resolution.

B3. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a manager, or pushed back

  • Competency: Healthy pushback, communication, and judgment.
  • STAR: [ADD DETAIL: no confirmed manager disagreement in the input — add facts before using this in an interview]. Situation: manager proposed a direction you thought was risky or wrong. Task: speak up without being insubordinate. Action: how you framed your concern, brought data or alternatives. Result: what happened (they changed course, or you executed their call and learned why it was right).
  • Spoken draft: Example skeleton: "My manager wanted to [decision]. I was concerned about [risk]. I brought [data or alternative], and we talked through trade-offs. We decided to [outcome]. It taught me [when to push vs. when to trust and execute]."
  • Don't: Say you never disagree, or frame it as you vs. them.

B4. Tell me about a tough deadline, or a time you had to deliver through ambiguity

  • Competency: Execution under pressure, prioritization, and autonomy.
  • STAR: Situation: unclear requirements or tight timeline. Task: ship something valuable despite constraints. Action: how you scoped, communicated, and prioritized. Result: what you delivered and impact.
  • Spoken draft: "At Highspot, I built the LLM gateway under a tight timeline with evolving requirements. I worked with product to lock down the must-haves, built an MVP with rate limiting and logging first, and iterated based on early usage. We shipped on time, and it became the foundation for multiple AI features." (Alternatively, use the Figma-to-code pipeline at Article if that had deadline pressure.)
  • Don't: Say you just worked harder or blame the ambiguity without showing how you handled it.

B5. Tell me about your proudest achievement, or a time you led without formal authority

  • Competency: Impact, initiative, and influence.
  • STAR: Situation: a high-value project or cross-team effort. Task: drive it without being the manager. Action: how you rallied people, made decisions, and unblocked. Result: measurable outcome.
  • Spoken draft: "At Article, I led the Figma-to-code pipeline that powered over ten production apps. I didn't manage anyone, but I coordinated with design, product, and other engineers to agree on the API, built the core transformation logic in TypeScript, and wrote docs so other teams could onboard. It cut handoff time [ADD DETAIL: by X% or from Y to Z days if known] and became the standard toolchain."
  • Don't: Pick something with no measurable result, or take credit for someone else's work.
